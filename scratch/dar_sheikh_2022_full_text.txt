Abstract 
Jammu and Kashmir is well known for accommodating people of diverse 
ethnicities that differ on the basis of language, culture and religion. Apart 
from major speech communities like Kashmiri and Dogri, there are some 
minority speech communities as well living in different parts of Jammu and 
Kashmir. The minority communities in this region comprise of Pahari,  
Gaddi, Gojri, Poguli, Tibetan etc. Owing to the fact, this entire region is 
linguistically and culturally divergent. One of the minority communities of 
the region is Gaddi speeh community of Jammu region. The present paper 
aims to present the different linguistic and cultural domains like language, 
background of the community, basic vocabulary and kinship terminology of 
Gaddi speech community settled in B haderwah area of District Doda of 
Jammu and Kashmir. To carry the stu dy, a fieldwork was conducted in 
different villages of Bhaderwah area and data was collected through 
questionnaire method, interview method and observational method. 
Keywords: Gaddi, linguistic diversity, kinship, vocabulary 
1. Introduction 
The upper belt and some neighbouring areas of the Bhaderwah tehsil of 
the district Doda of Jammu and Kashmir are home to the Gaddi sp eech 
community. Some of them have settled in the Jammu region's Udhampur, 
Bani and Kathua areas. Originally, Gaddis belonged to the region of 
Chamba, but some of them migrated to the region of Bhaderwah over time. 
There are several reasons for their migratio n. But the harsh winters and 
the persecution by the Mughal emperors are said to be the key reasons for 
their migration, as stated by some of the Gaddi people. Gaddi speakers 
can be found in as many as ten villages in Bhaderwa h. The total 
population of Gaddis in Jammu d ivision is around 46,400  as per the 
census of 2011.

Drew has talked about Gaddi community in his book The Jummoo and 
Kashmir Territories: A Geographical Account.  He says, “The south-east 
end of Bhaderwah borders with the Chamba country, and the Gaddis 
living in Bhaderwah seem to have migrated at some time or other through 
Chamba Hills. They are Hindus and have the same subdivisions of caste 
as the others”. (1875: 107) 
2. Background 
The word Gaddi, meaning shepherd, is derived from the Sanskrit word 
Gaddar which means sheep. Gaddi is a semi-pastoral and semi-cultivating 
tribe. Originally Gaddis belonged to the Chamba region . Some of these 
have migrated to Bhaderwah around two hundred years ago. There are as 
many as ten villages in Bhaderwah where Gaddi speakers are found.  
“Most of the population works as shepherds, The Gaddi people are locally 
called by the name Ghadeshi which means “the people from the country 
of shepherds.”  Cattle rearing is considered as the main occupation of 
Gaddi people, thus, no matter how rich or poor a per son is, everyone 
among the Gaddi community respects this occupation. The person having 
large number of cattle is considered as rich among the community. Cattle 
rearing is considered as respectable profession as it is the profession of 
their ancestors. As the Gaddi people live in the remote area they have 
comparatively less facilities than other communities; they cannot avail 
their food items very easily so they mostly prefer to grow different crops 
on their own soil. People belonging to the community, who have migrated 
from higher region to the main city of Bhaderwah, have taken up different 
jobs to earn their livelihood. They are also enjoying the reservation in the 
appointment of government jobs” (Sheikh & Dar, 2021: 49-70).  
3. Religion 
The Gaddi community follows Hinduism. The community, in spite of its 
contact with Muslims, has been able to retain and hold on to their cultural 
beliefs and norms with little or no modification. The religious belief of 
the Gaddi people revolves around the faith in Lord Shiva. While 
worshipping Lord Shiva is almost a common practice in Hinduism, Gaddi 
people differ from other Hindus in their belief towards lord Shiva. They 
believe that only Gaddis were allowed by the Lord Shiva to sit on his seat 
and that seat is called as Gaddi and they are the descendants of Lord Shiva. 
This belief make them the special disciples of Lord Shiva. A good number 
of festivals are celebrated by Gaddi people throughout the year and every 
festival has its own significance and importance.

Shiv Ratri (the birthday celebration of Lord Shiva) is their main festival. 
They also participate in the festivals of other religious communities like 
Muslims as the community is surrounded by Muslims. It depicts the 
communal harmony prevalent in the community. Muslims also participate 
in the festivals of their Hindu brothers.  
4.  Language 
Gaddi is an I ndo-Aryan language. Bailey (190 8) classified Gaddi as a 
language of Western Pahari group of Indo -Aryan languages in his book 
The Languages of Northern Himalayas . The word Pahari applies to the 
group of languages spoken in the Sub -Himalayan hills extending from 
Bhaderwah to the eastern parts of the Nepal.  
As mentioned above Gaddi is an Indo -Aryan language. It belongs to the 
Western Pahari Sub -group. The family tr ee of Gaddi language is as 
follows (Ethnologue 2014).

Gaddi is also known by other names , such as, Bharmauri, Bhadi, 
Gaddyali, Gadiali, Bharmauri. However, Gaddi is the most common name 
used by the speakers of the language.

4.1.5  Kinship 
The system which is defined by the relationships between individuals 
having the family ties either in terms of biological relations or culturally 
constructed relations is called as kinship system. The kinship systems vary 
in different forms of social organization and each community has its own 
set of the terms which forms its kinship terminology.

Kinship is defined as “the system of social relationships connecting 
people in a culture who are or are held to be related and defining and 
regulating their reciprocal obligations.” (Gladwin,1948: 73-94). Kinship 
shows relatedness between individuals or groups formulated by blood or 
by the bond of marriage. It can sometimes also be formulated through the 
process of adaptation in which one individual is related to another neither 
by blood nor by the marital affinity but are procreated by adapting a child. 
According to Brown (1952:280) “kinship is a system of dyadic relations 
between person and person in a community, the behavior of any two 
persons in any of these relations is regulated in some way and to a greater 
or less extent by social usage”.

4.1.5.1.1 Kinship Terminology of Gaddi Language 
The Gaddi language has its own collection of kinship terms for both 
affinal and consanguineal relationships. Most relationships are denoted by 
distinct terms, but some relationships are denoted by a common term, as 
all male cousins are typically referred to by a /ba:ji/ term, and a /dadda:/ 
term refers to all female cousins. The relationships set by blood and 
marriage in Gaddi community can be modelled as: 
 I. Consanguineal Relationships : The relationships which are set by 
blood fully or partially are consanguineal relationships. These relationships 
are classified on hierarchical basis from generation to generation. 
Consanguineal relationships comprises of: 
 a.  Own Generation 
The relationships of an individual's own generation include his or her 
brothers, sisters, and all first cousins. The relationships of one generation 
in the Gaddi group have a common term for brother and all male cousins

Conclusion 
Gaddi speech community of Jammu has remained the mystery to many as 
the no one has provided any description related to the cultural, linguistic 
and other aspects of the community. Although Gaddis are scattered in 
many parts of the country. But a good number of population is settled in 
the hilly terrains spread over the Chenab region of Jammu. The paper is 
an attempt to provide the detailed description of different cultural aspects 
of the Community. In this paper the researchers have given the description 
of the settlement, language and kinship system of the Gaddi speech 
community. It is hoped that this paper will pave way to carry more studies 
on this so far ignored community.
