## Executive Summary

Building a genuine, verifiable dictionary and computational language library for the **Gaddi language as spoken in Jammu & Kashmir (J&K)** requires primary linguistic evidence from speakers native to the Jammu hill districts. 

Following the strict baseline audit of Command 3—which quarantined 25 cultural and regional ecological terms and maintained `verified_jk_lexical_entries` strictly at **0**—this investigation conducted an exhaustive survey of published dictionaries, technical grammars, institutional repositories, national doctoral archives, and linguistic surveys.

### Principal Findings

1. **No Standalone J&K Gaddi Dictionary Located in Systematic Search (`not_located_in_search`):**  
   Systematic query across national repositories, academic databases, publisher lists, and cultural academies located **no published, dedicated descriptive dictionary or standalone grammar** for the Gaddi variety of Jammu & Kashmir. Neither state cultural academies, university linguistics departments, nor commercial publishers have issued a bilingual dictionary for J&K Gaddi in surveyed catalogues.
2. **The Strongest Published Research Candidate (Kaul, 2006):**  
   The primary published research candidate for localized linguistic documentation of J&K hill dialects is **Dr. Pritam Krishen Kaul's two-volume work *Pahāṛi and Other Tribal Dialects of Jammu* (Eastern Book Linkers, Delhi, 2006)**. Undertaken as a UGC Major Research Project, Volume 1 provides survey and structural analysis; Volume 2 compiles regional glossaries (Padri, Bhalesi, Cinali; verified: lacks Gaddi vocabulary).
   - **Digital Access Status (`digitized_record_found`):** A digitized record exists in Google Books (`https://books.google.com/books?id=pHZOAQAAIAAJ`, Volume 1). However, public access is restricted to **Snippet view** ('No eBook available', partial TOC displaying 3 entries with '7 other sections not shown'); no Gaddi linguistic pages are readable online.
   - **Physical Holdings Status (`institutional_confirmation_required`):** Catalogues list holdings at Dhanvantri Central Library (Jammu), Sahitya Akademi (New Delhi), National Library (Kolkata), and Library of Congress, but none of these physical shelves have been verified by direct inspection.
3. **Critical State Survey Omission (PLSI Vol. 12):**  
   The *People's Linguistic Survey of India* (PLSI), Volume 12 (*The Languages of Jammu & Kashmir*, Orient Blackswan), **completely omitted Gaddi** from its survey of 16 regional languages, despite the 2011 Census recording 24,161 mother-tongue Gaddi speakers in J&K. Gaddi was treated solely in Volume 11 (*The Languages of Himachal Pradesh*), leaving J&K Gaddi officially undocumented in national state-level surveys.
4. **Shodhganga Repository Audit:**  
   A systematic query of the INFLIBNET Shodhganga national thesis repository confirmed that **zero doctoral dissertations or M.Phil. theses in Linguistics, Phonetics, or Lexicography** exist for J&K Gaddi. The only completed theses covering Gaddi in J&K belong to Sociology (Panjab University, 2014) and Education (University of Jammu), providing sociological descriptions rather than descriptive linguistic lexicons.
5. **Rigorous Evidence Separation:**  
   All contemporary descriptive linguistic monographs (such as *A Grammar of Gaddi*, UCL Press, 2026) and historical surveys (Grierson, 1916; Bailey, 1908) describe varieties spoken in **Himachal Pradesh (Kangra and Chamba)**. In accordance with strict anti-conflation rules, they serve as high-quality **comparative baselines**, but cannot be relabeled as J&K Gaddi without independent local verification.

#### Detailed Content & Linguistic Methodology Analysis: Volume 1 vs. Volume 2

To maintain linguistic integrity, the two volumes must be evaluated independently:

1. **Volume 1 (Linguistic Survey & Analysis):**
   - Contains the analytical fieldwork, structural classifications, and comparative chapters resulting from the UGC research project.
   - Proposes a tripartite taxonomy of Jammu mountain speech:
     - *Western Pahari:* Dialects closely related to the Central/Western Pahari group of Himachal Pradesh (including Bhaderwahi and Gaddi contact varieties).
     - *Lehnda / Geographical Pahari:* Dialects displaying Western Punjabi (Lahnda) strata (e.g., Mirpuri, Poonchi, Pahari-Pothwari varieties).
     - *Dardo-Pahari:* Archaic contact varieties displaying transitional traits between Western Pahari and Kashmiri/Dardic.
   - Includes dedicated chapters examining **tribal, nomadic, and caste dialects**:
     - *Gojri* and *Bakerwali* (transhumant pastoralists).
     - *Gaddi* (transhumant shepherds of the Dhaula Dhar / Pir Panjal slopes).
     - *Watali* and *Cinnali (Chinali)* (newly documented marginalized varieties).
   - **Open Empirical Question for Physical Inspection:** Does Volume 1 provide systematic IPA/Devanagari wordlists, recorded oral specimens, and inflectional paradigms specifically elicited from Gaddi informants settled in J&K, or does it confine its treatment of Gaddi to ethnographic and sociolinguistic survey narrative? This question can only be resolved by direct physical page examination.

2. **Volume 2 (Reference Compendium & Glossaries):**
   - Functions strictly as a companion reference compendium compiling previously published research papers, historical documents, and specific localized glossaries.
   - Bibliographic analysis confirms the exact contents of Volume 2:
     - Detailed study and text specimens of **Padri (Padderi)**.
     - Descriptive notes and texts on **Bhalesi**.
     - A comprehensive **Glossary of Cinali (Chinali)**.
     - Reprints of Dr. Kaul's earlier papers on Chenab Valley antiquities.
   - **Crucial Anti-Conflation Finding:** **Volume 2 contains NO dedicated Gaddi vocabulary, glossary, or dictionary.** Volume 1's contents CANNOT be inferred from Volume 2.

##### Step 4: The 6-Point Lexical Extraction Verification Gate
Before any lemma found in Kaul (2006) Volume 1 may be entered into `data/lexicon_jk.json`, it must pass all six verification gates:
1. **Primary Attestation:** The word must appear printed in Volume 1; no conjectured forms.
2. **Explicit Linguistic Label:** The text must explicitly identify the form as *Gaddi* (or *Bharmauri* as spoken in Jammu), NOT Dogri, Bhaderwahi, Padri, or general Pahari.
3. **Geographical Localization:** The form must be traceable to a specific J&K district, tehsil, or settlement (e.g., Doda, Bhaderwah, Bani, Ramnagar).
4. **Precise Citation:** The exact volume, chapter, section, and page number must be recorded.
5. **Orthographic / Phonetic Fidelity:** The form must be transcribed exactly as printed, preserving original script, tone accents, and diacritics.
6. **Anti-Conflation Isolation:** Regional cultural terms common to all Jammu hill communities must not be classified as distinctive Gaddi language words unless attested as active Gaddi dialect vocabulary.

## 4. Alternate Names, Variety Designations, and Glottonyms

Accurate language documentation requires rigorous disambiguation of alternate names and orthographies across the Jammu-Himachal borderlands:

### 4.1 Glottonyms Evaluated
- **Gaddi (`gad` / `bfi`):**  
  The autonym used by community members. In administrative records, it frequently conflates the ethnic Scheduled Tribe designation with the linguistic mother tongue.
- **Bharmauri / Bharmouri:**  
  Historically rooted in the Bharmaur wazarat of Chamba State, Himachal Pradesh. Often applied in comparative linguistics to the entire language cluster, but must not be assumed to be identical to the varieties spoken by Gaddis settled for generations in Kathua or Doda.
- **Gaddiyali / Gaddi-Pahari:**  
  A common regional designation in the Jammu hills, emphasizing its Western Pahari structural characteristics and distinguishing it from Dogri.
- **Bhaderwahi (`bhd`) vs. Gaddi:**  
  Bhaderwahi is an independent Western Pahari language spoken natively in the Bhaderwah valley of Doda. While Gaddis living in Bhaderwah (e.g., Haddal, Assar) are fluent bilinguals and share regional cultural terms (*Nawala*, *Chinj*, *Pattu*), Gaddi maintains distinct nominal case markers, verbal agreement, and phonological features. Conflating Bhaderwahi with Gaddi is linguistically invalid.
- **Pahari / Western Pahari:**  
  A broad genetic classification covering dozens of distinct languages across Himachal Pradesh and Jammu. Merging varieties under the umbrella label "Pahari" obscures crucial dialectal boundaries.

1. **Dhanvantri Central Library & Department of Dogri**  
   University of Jammu, Baba Saheb Ambedkar Road, Jammu Tawi, Jammu & Kashmir 180006, India.  
   *Holdings:* Regional linguistic monographs, local dialect surveys, P. K. Kaul publications, unpublished M.Phil. dissertations.
2. **Jammu & Kashmir Academy of Art, Culture and Languages (JKAACL)**  
   Canal Road, Jammu Tawi, Jammu & Kashmir 180001, India.  
   *Holdings:* Print archives of *Sheeraza*, folklore collections, recordings of *Anchli* ballads.
3. **Sahitya Akademi Library**  
   Rabindra Bhavan, 35 Ferozeshah Road, New Delhi 110001, India.  
   *Holdings:* Comprehensive collection of Indian regional linguistic publications, Eastern Book Linkers editions, Western Pahari literature.
4. **Central Institute of Indian Languages (CIIL) & LDC-IL**  
   Hunsur Road, Manasagangotri, Mysuru, Karnataka 570006, India.  
   *Holdings:* National linguistic corpora, LDC-IL speech and parallel text databases.
5. **Panjab University Library**  
   Sector 14, Chandigarh 160014, India.  
   *Holdings:* Complete print archive of Ashwani Kumar (2014) and related Himalayan sociology/anthropology dissertations.
